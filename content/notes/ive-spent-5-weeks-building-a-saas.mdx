Seven weeks ago, I had an idea to create a Shopify clone for African brick-and-mortar businesses. The stars had aligned, and everything just made sense, but now I needed to know one thing;

“Would people actually pay for this thing…?”

So I did what any builder would do and reached out to my consultant, a clanker known as ChatGPT, and together, we devised a plan to conduct extensive market research.

Fast forward a couple of weeks. I had my ideal persona, a Typeform clone I hacked together for my specific use case, and a constitution-sized conversation with ChatGPT that understood my context.

Responses to the dynamic survey were stored in a Google Sheet, which I fed to Gemini to analyze for trends.

Now at this point, I was juggling four tools; ChatGPT, Gemini, my Typeform clone, and my brain, all for market research.

It worked, But there was one major flaw.

Outside my head, none of these tools were connected.

ChatGPT had all the context. Typeform had the survey questions without context. Google Sheets had the answers without the questions *nor* context. And my two brain cells were holding everything together manually, with the third one cheerleading.

There was no single, seamless tool that helped startups, product teams, and growing businesses conduct market research without the chaos.

That’s when my brain child was born: Geniy.
