Imagine giving an AI model digital arms and legs (well, that’s just an agent), but after the release of Manus, no other agent has got me as excited as GrokBot has in the last month I’ve been using it. It only came out on August 11, but the only limitations I’ve had for its use cases were just a matter of expanding my own creativity.

Walk with me.

I think we’ve reached a point where, to the average techie, improvements on AI models have plateaued in terms of wow-factor. For instance, although a model like Claude Mythos is really capable- to the point where the US government had to get involved- it didn’t really feel as impactful to the regular person upon the release of Fable 5 (Mythos’ younger brother who just wanted to play).

You can’t say you were impressed; it was more of a ‘huh, I see. What else is new?’ moment. This is because our minds can’t grasp the difference in AI benchmark scores. Now, the things that impress me in regard to Artificial Intelligence are the ability of these models to perform tasks, not like picking up a coffee or something, but a task I would give to my PA, and performing quite well while they’re at it. This blows my mind in more ways than a Fable 5.9 release ever could.

This is why GrokBot fascinates me.

Now, before you begin using GrokBot, there’s a mindset shift you’ve got to make.

> “You’re not just talking to a model; you’re talking to an assistant that can perform complex digital tasks you’d usually feel comfortable giving to a person. So by extension, you’re talking to a human with a laptop.”

When I ask AI to do something, honestly, I don’t expect it to tell me how to do it (I would have done it otherwise); what I need is for it to get going and get it done. Grokbot understands this.

“Every morning, check my Google Calendar for today’s events. Using current traffic conditions, work out when I should leave the house, and send me that time along with a Google Maps link with the route already loaded up so I can just tap and go. While you’re at it, throw together a Spotify playlist to drive to.”

GrokBot: “Sure, would you want some fries with that?”

“Open my [X] repo and add [Y change]. Commit it to a new branch, open a PR, and once Jenkins passes, merge it and confirm the new build is live on AWS.”

GrokBot: “lightweight”

“Send Adwoa a text reminding her how much I love her at precisely 8:42 am every morning… 😅Make no mistakesss”

GrokBot: “Sure, I’ll let you know if she ever comes around”

I wasn’t kidding when I said your imagination is the limit, and four things make it so intuitive to use.

### The Agent Computer

If you’ve used Manus before, you know what I’m talking about. Essentially, every GrokBot account comes with a Linux virtual machine + GUI provisioned alongside after signup.

This means that when you give it a task, it has a computer with a browser, terminal and file manager, allowing it to connect to the internet, execute Linux commands and store files. So you can tell it to do something, and it would take a screenshot of its work on its computer and show it to you.

And the amazing bit is, every agent you create has access to this same computer, so you can reference tasks and files other bots left off and pick up from there with a new bot.

### Routines

Routines are a simple concept but super effective when done right. With GrokBot, routines are built into the conversation.

Just tell it.

“Every day at 7:30, go online and search for internship opportunities that match my resume. Then share the links with me so I can apply.”

That’s it. No YAML. No conditional logic statements. It creates the routine, and keeps running even when your laptop is closed.

Clocks aren’t the only trigger though. You can hook it up to notifications from third party apps like GitHub, Slack, or whatever. The Bot wakes up because something happened, not just because a schedule said so.

### Speak to it like a person

This seems obvious but remember how we’ve been told to prompt over the last two years.

“Be concise.” “Don’t make mistakes.” “You are a 10x senior engineer at Amazon who NEVER hallucinates.” Most tools have you doing mental gymnastics.

GrokBot doesn’t need all that. Go straight to the point, again as though you were texting an actual person.

Talk to it like someone competent on your team. Correct it when it’s slightly off. It remembers. Next time it’s closer. A few turns in, it understands the way you prefer your work done which then propagates into other bots you’ve got set up.

### Integrations

First-party plugins take care of the apps you mostly use; your Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Slack, GitHub, Notion, and friends. You authorize once, and every agent on your account can use that connection.

Third-party is the other half. Through the marketplace, MCP, and the browser, you can link in tools that aren’t on the official list, or just have it work a site the way you would when there’s no clean API. You’re not waiting for a connector before the Bot gets useful.

So “check my mail,” “update the sheet,” “watch this Slack channel” isn’t for show. It’s your actual accounts. That’s also why approvals matter. Jarvis is fun until Jarvis texts your lecturer by accident.

Set up properly, this is the closest we’ve come to a functional, real-world JARVIS.
